# Data profile: the Kaggle truckload dataset

All counts were computed from `data/kaggle_logistics/` on 2026-10-10. The dataset is **synthetic**: a fictional
carrier generated by its author from industry patterns, not records of a real company.

## In one paragraph

A US over-the-road truckload carrier, 2022–2024: about **78 loads a day** on **58 lanes between 20 cities**, each
load a full truckload from one shipper to one receiver, dry van or refrigerated, with revenue, a booking type
(spot, contract, dedicated) and pickup and delivery appointments. The fleet is 120 trucks, 180 trailers and 150
drivers based at 24 home terminals.

## The freight

- **Volume:** 54–108 loads a day, median 78; 28,165–28,656 a year.
- **Lanes:** all 58 lanes are used, each by 1,386–1,628 loads. Busiest: Columbus → Philadelphia (1,628),
  Philadelphia → New York (1,537), Columbus → Los Angeles (1,534).
- **Length of haul:** median 1,271 loaded miles (10th–90th percentile 519–2,561). Typical transit 1–5 days.
- **Equipment:** 50% refrigerated, 50% dry van. Weight median 27,482 lb (13,470–41,456).
- **Money:** revenue per load median $2,828 (median $2.20 per loaded mile); 62% of loads carry accessorial charges
  (median $100).
- **Booking type:** dedicated 50%, contract 25%, spot 25%.
- **Appointments:** every pickup is scheduled on the load's date, between 06:00 and 18:00. Scheduled pickup →
  delivery: median 24 h (10th–90th percentile 9.5–46 h).
- **Imbalance:** some cities receive far more than they send, so trucks must drive empty to reload. Los Angeles
  receives 8,948 more loads than it sends over three years; Indianapolis 5,810, Denver 4,358. Columbus, Phoenix and
  Chicago send about 4,400–4,650 more than they receive.

## What looks generated (do not model from these)

- **No seasonality or weekly rhythm:** every calendar month has 6,582–7,339 loads, and Saturday and Sunday are as
  busy as weekdays. (The Kaggle description mentions Q4 peaks; the data has none.)
- **Flat prices:** spot, contract and dedicated all have the same median $2.20 per mile.
- **Flat customers and lanes:** the 10 biggest customers hold only 5.6% of loads; lane volumes are nearly equal.
- **Service and detention:** on-time is 67% at pickup and 45% at delivery; 87% of stops record detention (median
  90 min at pickup, 120 at delivery). The description claims 85–95% on time.
- **Facilities do not match stops:** for 97% of stops, the facility named on the event is in a different city than
  the stop itself.
- **Truck histories are impossible** (the reason `trips` is not imported): only 4.6% of a truck's next pickups
  are in the city of its last delivery; 55% of consecutive loads on the same truck overlap in time; 486 loads
  deliver before they are picked up; trucks average 19.8 long-haul loads a month, about 2–3 times what a truck
  can drive.

## How the simulator uses it

- **Used as given:** the load stream (date, lane, equipment, weight, revenue, booking type, scheduled
  appointments), lanes and their miles, rates and transit days, city coordinates, fleet size, trailer types and
  home terminals.
- **Produced by the simulator, not taken from the data:** where every truck is, empty miles to the next pickup,
  driving time, whether the appointment is met, and therefore lateness and detention.
- **Assumptions we must add and state:** when a load becomes known to the dispatcher (the data has no tender
  time); hours-of-service rules; what happens to loads the fleet cannot cover (decline or broker out).
- **Capacity:** about 78 loads a day at a median of 1,271 miles is more than 120 trucks can haul (roughly twice
  as much), so accepting or declining loads is part of every dispatch decision.
